# Blitzy pre-sales positioning directives

Last updated: October 20, 2026. Refresh when pre-sales positioning or engagement guidance changes.

Source: Supplied notes from the October 16, 2026 AISC training, “How to land deals without a POV.” These directives summarize the training; they do not establish contractual terms or verified product capabilities.

Position Blitzy around changing how the customer delivers software. Use individual initiatives to demonstrate and adopt that process.

**Applicability:** Use these directives for early sales positioning toward a paid engagement while bypassing a POV or technical validation. Once a workstream is in an active POV or technical validation, use `shared-context/blitzy_pov_playbook.md` to guide its outputs, deliverables and feedback against the applicable playbook criteria and customer-agreed evaluation. These directives can inform a separate expansion conversation; they do not replace active evaluation commitments. Apply this distinction per workstream, as described in `AGENTS.md`.

1. **Lead discovery with strategic priorities and delivery processes.**
   Establish what the organization needs to change, where software delivery constrains it, and what leadership expects from AI adoption. Gather stack details and timelines once their relevance is clear.

2. **Distinguish project, program and process positioning.**
   A conversion is a project; modernizing a system is a program; changing how teams define, build and validate software is process transformation. Connect the immediate work to the broader opportunity when the customer’s needs support that connection.

3. **Find a sponsor who can authorize changes to how teams work.**
   Engage executives, architecture leaders and engineering leaders responsible for outcomes across initiatives. Give delivery teams concrete answers about their responsibilities and technical concerns.

4. **Qualify readiness for change early.**
   Determine whether the customer will adjust planning, review gates, team participation and execution practices. Establish whether the sponsor has the authority and resources to support those changes.

5. **Make the proposed way of working explicit.**
   Explain how enterprise context, human-defined intent, plan review, autonomous execution and validation fit together. Show what the customer owns, what Blitzy handles and where approval remains necessary.

6. **Demonstrate the workflow and the decisions it enables.**
   Walk through how people understand a system, define an outcome, review a plan and assess delivered work. Use a standard demonstration when sufficient; tailor it when a specific concern requires direct evidence.

7. **Propose a paid adoption pilot as the default commercial path.**
   Frame the engagement around adopting and measuring a repeatable delivery process. The training suggests a six-to-twelve-month window; agree the duration, scope, support and commercial terms with the customer.

8. **Keep sandbox evaluation bounded.**
   Use sandbox access to answer defined questions about the platform experience or technical fit. Establish what decision it supports and how it connects to the paid engagement.

9. **Agree the minimum viable process change.**
   Start with changes the customer can realistically adopt. Define the initial participants, review responsibilities, support and success measures before proposing broader organizational adoption.

10. **Use several distinct initiatives to test broader applicability.**
    Where appropriate, select three tracks across separate teams—for example, modernization, brownfield improvements and greenfield development. Choose work that tests different needs and produces useful customer outcomes.

11. **Use the customer’s language for business initiatives.**
    Connect discussions to their products, business units and strategic priorities. Keep individual refactors and projects concrete while explaining how the resulting capability could support subsequent work.

12. **Address workflow friction directly.**
    If the customer expects Blitzy to fit unchanged into existing sprint or task practices, discuss the execution model and necessary adjustments. Determine whether there is a workable adoption path rather than promising compatibility that has not been established.

13. **Measure process adoption alongside technical delivery.**
    Agree how to assess preparation effort, review effort, validation, delivery time and reuse across initiatives. A successful implementation should also inform whether the team can repeat the approach.

14. **Keep claims proportional to the evidence.**
    Treat the training’s customer-acquisition figures as an internal observation, not proof that skipping a POV causes faster sales. Support customer-facing claims with applicable evidence and explicit assumptions.

15. **Share effective discovery and positioning practices internally.**
    Capture useful questions, objections, responses and demonstration approaches. Keep shared examples redacted and suitable for reuse.
