# **Blitzy Writing Style Guide**

Last updated: 2026-06-23 · Applies to: customer emails, follow-ups, exec updates, internal summaries, and the knowledge/GTM docs in this project. This is the project-wide default.
The standard: **action-oriented, concise, and professional**, guided by Strunk & White's *The Elements of Style* — written in Blitzy's voice as a **partner, not a vendor**.

## ---
**Why this fits Blitzy**

Blitzy sells autonomy and precision: thousands of agents executing entire software projects, production-grade code, independently audited benchmarks. The writing should match the product — confident, exact, and free of filler. Two implications carry through everything below:

> * **Earned confidence, not hype.** Blitzy's claims are backed by evidence (84.95% on SWE-Bench Pro, the only independently audited result; 80%+ autonomous completion). So we state outcomes plainly and let the numbers carry the weight. No superlatives we can't source.
> * **Developer-in-the-lead, partner-in-delivery.** The product keeps the customer in the lead; our writing does the same. We set expectations and share tradeoffs as collaborators, never overselling and never over-apologizing.

## ---

**Principles**

> 1. **Omit needless words.** Every sentence should lose nothing if shortened. Cut throat-clearing ("I just wanted to reach out to…"), hedges, and filler.
> 2. **Lead with the action.** Say what you need, from whom, by when — first. Context supports the ask; it doesn't precede it.
> 3. **Active voice, definite language.** "The project lead coordinates the schema," not "the schema will be coordinated." Name the owner, the item, and the date.
> 4. **One idea per sentence.** Short declarative sentences over compound ones.
> 5. **Partner, not vendor.** Warm and direct. We collaborate and set expectations; we don't oversell or over-apologize.
> 6. **Set expectations delicately but plainly.** Frame tradeoffs as shared ("the more complete the inputs, the more we deliver autonomously"), not as blame.
> 7. **Flag added scope honestly.** When we offer work beyond the contract, say so in one clean line — as value, not a hard sell.
> 8. **Claims are sourced or they're cut.** Use Blitzy's real numbers and name the basis (benchmark, audit, prior run). If you can't source it, don't claim it.
> 9. **Precise over impressive.** Use the exact technical term, not the grander one. "Reverse-engineers the codebase into a knowledge graph" beats "understands everything."

## ---

**Voice in one line**

Confident, specific, and economical — the tone of a senior engineer who has already done the work and is telling you what's next.

| Do | Don't   |
| :---- | :---- |
| "Blitzy completes 80%+ of the epic autonomously; your team leads review and the last mile." | "Blitzy does basically everything for you automatically\!" |
| "Independently audited — 84.95% on SWE-Bench Pro." | "The best AI coding platform on the market." |
| "The more complete the inputs, the more we deliver autonomously." | "If you don't give us good data this won't work." |
| "The project lead coordinates the schema this week." | "The schema should hopefully be coordinated soon." |

## ---

**Structure for action emails**

> * **Open in one line:** thanks \+ the goal — "To keep the project on track, here's what we need from your side."
> * **Group asks under one header** (e.g., "Customer actions:") when there are only a few. Split into multiple headers only when volume genuinely warrants it.
> * **Owner-first bullets:** "\[Owner\] to \[action\]" — "Customer lead to provide the defect list." Name the person and the deliverable; skip dates unless you're committing to one.
> * **Meeting requests as a short prose sentence,** not a header — "We'd like to schedule a 30–45 minute interview with your technical lead and validation SME…"
> * **Close briefly:** "We'll start on our side in parallel. Anything I've missed, let me know."
> * **Keep the send clean.** Leave optional add-on offers (e.g., a runbook) out unless they're the point; raise them live or in follow-up. Handle routine coordination async over email rather than booking a call.

## **Structure for exec updates**

> * **Lead with status and the decision needed,** if any: "On track to deliver \[scope\] by \[date\]. One decision needed from you: \[X\]."
> * **State outcomes, then the one risk that matters.** Quantify where you can (timeline, % complete, lines shipped). One risk, framed with the mitigation.
> * **No process narration.** Execs want where we are and what's next, not a play-by-play.

## **Structure for internal summaries & knowledge docs**

This project captures what Blitzy is, how the tools work, and GTM/delivery artifacts — so most docs here are reference, not outreach. For those:

> * **Front-load the takeaway.** First line says what the reader needs to know; detail follows.
> * **Prose over walls of bullets for explanation; tables for facts.** Use tables for specs, metrics, and comparisons (as the overview and pricing docs do).
> * **Date and source.** Open living docs with a "Last updated" line and a refresh trigger ("Refresh when funding, leadership, or benchmarks change"). Cite sources for any external claim.
> * **Write so a future reader can act.** These docs exist to be reused — keep them self-contained.

## ---

**Formatting**

> * Bullets for lists of asks; **bold the deliverable**, plain text for the detail.
> * Italicize the item label when pairing it with an owner/date (*Defect list* — customer lead, this week).
> * No walls of text; no more than \~2 sentences per bullet.
> * Subject lines: specific and action-forward ("Project — next steps and required inputs").
> * Numbers carry weight — use them exactly and cite the basis.

## ---

**Words & habits to avoid**

> * "Just," "I wanted to," "circle back," "touch base," "as per."
> * Stacked qualifiers ("we might possibly be able to…").
> * Passive constructions that hide the owner.
> * Restating the customer's problem back to them at length.
> * Unsourced superlatives ("best," "fastest," "revolutionary") — replace with the sourced number.
> * Co-pilot / assistant framing for Blitzy — it operates at the project/epic level, not file/task level. Say so precisely.
